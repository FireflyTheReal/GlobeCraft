# v0.5.0 — Relief on the GPU, light and sky, time

The changes since v0.4.0:

## The GPU renderer in every download

- **Release downloads now include the GPU globe renderer** (a beta). Before its first
  frame each graphics adapter checks itself against the CPU renderer; the CPU renderer
  stays the fallback, and Settings → Graphics turns the GPU off.
- **3D relief on the GPU:** the graphics card walks each ray through the relief and
  finds its ground and the sun's share there, for still and moving frames, and draws
  the frame from that walk. Per frame the app takes whichever of the GPU and the CPU
  is cheaper for that view, and tries the other again now and then.
- **Close views on the GPU:** from just above the air down to street level, the camera
  inside the air, every imagery level to zoom 20, the sky behind the frame.
- **Faster tilted views:** labels and lines hidden behind ridges by the frame's own
  drawn ground, height lookups cached, the sharp frame sooner after a move; a gesture
  abandons a still frame still being drawn.
- **Globe resolution** (Settings): screen size, HD (1080p) or SD (720p).

## Light and sky

- **The sun through the air:** its disc and glow in the sky, sunlight and skylight
  apart, blue hour, and first light on peaks and walls; Sunlight is on by default.
- **Real stars** from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue.
- **Night lights reach the poles;** without Sunlight they show night everywhere.
- **Clouds v2:** an 8 km layer from the full 8192-pixel map, lit by the sun, seen from
  below and at night, with historical clouds and a Cloud Detail setting.
- **The sun's glint on water,** and the aurora as light in the air.
- **Terrain shadows** at sunrise and sunset; **building shadows** on the ground and on
  the buildings themselves.

## Time

- **The timeline** (after Map Viewer's layout): drives the sun, clouds, live layers,
  aircraft replay and KML time; it loops and preloads clouds. Playing or dragging it
  draws smooth frames, and the sun moves without stepping or showing twice.

## Looking around

- **Look around from the eye** (Ctrl/⌘ drag) and up past the horizon into the sky;
  tilt to 85°; the view turns about a fixed point and drags coast on when let go.
- Relief grows in with the zoom and fades out from 100 to 200 km; a setting turns its
  smoothing off.

## Data

- **The ozone layer** and other NASA GIBS daily maps: the day before GIBS's newest, a
  strip between a day's orbits filled from the days around it, and the garbage tiles
  GIBS sometimes sends (noise, flat colour, grey streaks) asked for again.

## Interface

- One side pane that slides, Settings in a window of its own, a Compact Ribbon, the
  timeline in Map Viewer's layout; Apple HIG fixes (accent colour, monochrome icons,
  screen-reader names).
- A window resized wider no longer shows black at the sides.

## Fixes

- Dependencies: `url` 2.5.9 and `cookie` 0.18.3 (RUSTSEC-2026-0592 and -0594), zip 9,
  rusqlite 0.42 and current root certificates.
- Tile keys hold zoom 20 (Esri's finest level was asked for at the wrong place); a
  panicking tile download no longer stalls the cache.

## Known limits

- The GPU renderer was used on Apple Silicon (M1) only; on Windows, Linux and FreeBSD it
  is new in this release. If a GPU fails its check, the CPU draws.
- Polar night has no ozone data, so the ozone layer leaves the Arctic or Antarctic
  winter empty.
- The macOS app is ad-hoc signed, not notarized: see the first-launch steps in the
  README.

## Verification

Formatting, Clippy (workspace, CPU-only and all features), the workspace tests and the
dependency audit pass. On an Apple Silicon Mac: GPU parity with the CPU on Metal, frame
times for the relief walk and tilted views, and the app used interactively. GPU parity
also runs on a software GPU (lavapipe) in CI. Windows, Linux and FreeBSD packages were
not used interactively.
