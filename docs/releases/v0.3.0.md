# v0.3.0 — First GlobeCraft release

The first release from this repository, `FireflyTheReal/GlobeCraft` (formerly the
EarthCraft rewrite). The changes since v0.2.0:

## 3D terrain and tilt

- **Real relief.** Every view ray is marched through Mapterhorn elevation tiles,
  so mountains hide what lies behind them and stand against the sky up to the
  horizon (the earlier relief was a 2.5D displacement without occlusion that
  smeared imagery on tilted views). Hill shading makes ridges and valleys readable
  without sunlight; the exaggeration is adjustable (0.5×–3×).
- **Faster relief.** One cone per block of pixels clears the open air before the
  pixels march, and moving views over relief render at a lower resolution until
  the view rests: moving frames take about half the time of the first terrain build.
- **Tilt.** The camera looks at a point and can tilt up to 80°: right-drag or
  Shift+drag up and down, Shift+scroll, Shift+↑/↓, `U` for straight down. A tilted
  camera stays above the ground beneath it, and borders and names lie on the relief.
- **Tilt on the navigator.** An arc beside the navigator ring shows the tilt:
  drag its thumb or click the arc, double-click for straight down.

## Navigation

- A new **Globe** navigator (the default): a rotation ring with clickable
  N/E/S/W letters, a mini globe that marks the area in view and turns the Earth
  when dragged, and the zoom slider. The Google Earth-style navigator stays
  available as **Classic** (Settings → Navigation).
- Zooming keeps north and the heading; zooming in while tilted stops at the
  tilt's height instead of flattening it.

## Borders and names

- Country, state and municipal borders and place names now come from
  OpenStreetMap vector tiles (OpenFreeMap), streamed and cached as you zoom.
  Disputed borders are dashed, coasts stay visible on the night side, and names
  use English where available with installed fonts for other scripts.

## Imagery and loading

- **Historical imagery:** pick a Wayback date by year, and compare two dates with
  a swipe.
- **Night lights:** NASA Black Marble (2016) as a layer, shown on the night side
  with Sunlight on.
- **Smarter loading:** downloads of tiles the view has left are stopped, each tile
  source has its own download limit (two at once for OpenStreetMap, as its policy
  asks), and a server asking to slow down is respected. The coarser imagery a zoom
  out will show and a fly-to's destination are loaded ahead.
- Tile sources are described as data (`sources.json`), so a new source needs no
  new code.

## Sky

- **Stars** (on by default) and the **Milky Way** backdrop are separate switches.

## Screenshots

- Four qualities: Normal (screen size), Plus (2×), Pro (4×) and Pro Max (8×). All
  wait for every tile; Plus and above use one imagery level across the picture,
  so no seams show between tile tiers. Large pictures are rendered and written in
  strips on their own threads, so the globe stays responsive; *Cancel Screenshot*
  stops one.

## Fixes

- Tilted close-ups no longer freeze the globe (border dashes far off screen).
- Flight labels below 650 km no longer freeze the view.
- Moving and resting frames use the same imagery level on large windows.
- Sunlight redraws the view at once again.

## Known limits

- 3D terrain is drawn on the CPU. Overlays are raised onto the relief but not yet
  hidden behind ridges; the ruler, placemarks and live objects stay at sea level.
- The scale bar and the navigator's view disc assume a top-down view.
- GPU globe rendering remains experimental and opt-in; release downloads use the
  CPU renderer.
- The macOS app is ad-hoc signed, not notarized: see the first-launch steps in the
  README.

## Verification

Formatting, Clippy (workspace, CPU-only and all features) and tests run in CI on
Rust 1.97, and the release configuration is checked with Rust 1.95, the release
toolchain. Performance numbers are in [PERFORMANCE_BASELINE](../PERFORMANCE_BASELINE.md).
The app was tested interactively on an Apple Silicon Mac. Windows, Linux and
FreeBSD packages are built by the release workflow but were not tested on those
systems.
